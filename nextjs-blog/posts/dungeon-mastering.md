Running your first game as a **Dungeon Master** can feel daunting, but it's one of the most rewarding creative experiences you can have. You're not just telling a story — you're building a world that responds to your players' choices.

## Start Small

You don't need a sprawling continent with a thousand years of history. A single village, a nearby dungeon, and one looming threat is enough to run a great session.

## The Three Pillars

Every good D&D session balances:

- **Exploration** — letting players discover the world
- **Social interaction** — roleplaying with NPCs
- **Combat** — tactical, dramatic encounters

Lean into whichever pillar your table enjoys most, but don't neglect the others entirely.

## Preparation vs. Improvisation

Prepare situations, not scripts. If you know what the villain *wants* and what the world looks like *now*, you can improvise everything else on the fly.
